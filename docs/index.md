![MEGR 2156-7 Title](MEGR2156title.png)

This site is the engineering record for MEGR 2156/2157 Sophomore Design at UNC Charlotte: eleven assignments running from a single loaded bracket to a complete lead screw translating system.

**How it is organized.** Every assignment entry on this site is structured the same way — *Analyze* (the governing model, its variables, and the assumptions that make it valid), *Decide* (the alternatives considered, the criteria applied, and the selection made), *Communicate* (the drawings, figures, and write-up a colleague would receive). A reader who learns that structure once can navigate any entry on the site without relearning it.

**The standard this record holds itself to.** Every assignment entry will state the governing model and its variables, the numerical values and units used, the assumption that makes the model valid, and the alternative I rejected together with the criterion I rejected it on — so that a reader can recompute my result and challenge my choice without contacting me.

> **Engineering is the art of making decisions you can defend.**

Use the navigation on the left. Assignment entries are labelled by subject, not by number alone, so a specific piece of work can be located from the sidebar without opening pages in sequence.

## About Me

**Shamar Lindo**

I am a junior at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte. I am currently a mechanical
engineering student taking solid mechanics, thermodynamics, dynamic systems, and manufacturing
systems during this semester. I chose mechanical engineering as a major because I wanted to
understand things at a level where I could go and change different variables instead of just
operating them.

My best representation of my own engineering work through my three years at UNC Charlotte is
undergraduate research with Dr. Roger Tipton, where we extracted cellulose microfibers from North
Carolina hemp hurd for creating biocomposites. We worked on the extraction trials, where we treated
four particle sizes of hurd through a two-hour sodium hydroxide wash at 70 °C, stirring in a pot,
then two one-hour sodium chlorite bleaching treatments, then a thirty-minute room-temperature wash.
We oven-dried the fiber at 105 °C, and we used TGA/DTG and particle sizing by optical microscopy to
assess fiber purity.

Mill 99, the finest grade of hurd, produced the highest-purity fiber. It was about 1.5 g per 10 g of
raw hurd, and it was substantially harder to process. The sieve unders produced fiber of comparable
purity at about 3.5 g per 10 g, and we selected sieve unders. This was the first time I understood
that an engineering question isn't just the best fit; it's the number that survives under
constraints. We presented the work through UNC Charlotte's Office of Undergraduate Research.

The type of engineer I aspire to be works where a model has to survive contact with a budget and
real processes. I am pursuing internships in manufacturing, design, and energy engineering. What I
want most from the next two years is for my work to become something.

### What it means to defend an engineering decision

To defend a decision means being able to state why I didn't cut corners and the alternatives I
considered. It's not the same as just being confident or being right. A decision can be well defended
and still turn out badly; a decision can turn out well, but you are unable to defend it.

Do I know how to do it currently? I can defend the selection of sieve unders over mill 99 in the
extraction work. The criteria for this were fiber purity, mass yield, and processing difficulty
during filtration. The purity of these different mills was comparable through TGA/DTG, and yield
differed by roughly a factor of two. That decision I can reconstruct and argue with someone who
disagrees. What I cannot yet do is defend a decision in the moment as I make it, because I tend to
arrive at an answer first and then construct the justification afterwards. I also do not have the
habit of writing down rejected alternatives at the moment I reject them, so by the time I document
the work, they're not recorded for comparison. That is the specific gap I wish to close instead of
letting it go.
